## The printing offices {#offizinen}

The books are grouped here by printing office, by hand; books that share a title are told apart by year and edition.

### Johann Amerbach

Five volumes from the most learned Basel office – two editions of the same Zeitglöcklein and three devotional prints,
among them the *Bereitung zu dem Heiligen Sakrament*, printed together with Johann Petri de Langendorff and at 511 pages the largest volume in the corpus.

- [Bereitung zu dem Heiligen Sakrament (1489)](/incunabula/books/bereitung) — The largest volume: 511 pages and three marked re-uses.
- [Zeitglöcklein (1490)](/incunabula/books/zeitgloecklein-1490) — The first of the two Amerbach editions; two scans share one position.
- [Lob der Glieder Mariens (1492)](/incunabula/books/lob-der-glieder) — At 23 pages the smallest volume, bound together with the Zeitglöcklein (1492) and the Orationes.
- [Orationes (1492)](/incunabula/books/orationes) — A twenty-four-page book of prayers – one of the two shortest prints in the corpus.
- [Zeitglöcklein (1492)](/incunabula/books/zeitgloecklein-1492) — The second, much fuller edition – 402 pages, thirteen notes.

### Johann Bergmann de Olpe

Brant's publisher: the 1495 German edition of the Narrenschiff and the two Latin editions of 1497 – one image programme in three settings.

- [[Das] Narrenschiff (dt.) (1495)](/incunabula/books/narrenschiff-dt) — Brant's satire in the German edition of 1495, framed by border strips.
- [[Das] Narrenschiff (lat.) (March 1497)](/incunabula/books/narrenschiff-lat-maerz) — Locher's Latin translation, March 1497 – the same blocks, the text newly set.
- [[Das] Narrenschiff (lat.) (Aug. 1497)](/incunabula/books/narrenschiff-lat-august) — The second Latin edition of the same year, twenty-two pages longer.

### Michael Furter

Four prints across thirteen years, from the 1491 commentary on the pericopes to the little book of Methodius in 1504.

- [Postilla (1491)](/incunabula/books/postilla) — Furter's commentary on the pericopes; two blocks, each printed twice in the same volume.
- [Quadragesimale (1495)](/incunabula/books/quadragesimale) — Meder's Lenten sermons; 468 pages, 23 of them described.
- [Passio sancti Meynrhadi (1496)](/incunabula/books/passio-meynrhadi) — The Einsiedeln legend of St Meinrad, dated to the day.
- [Eyn biechlin sancti Methodij (1504)](/incunabula/books/methodij) — The latest print in the corpus and Furter's last print here.

### Lienhart Ysenhut

Three volumes around 1489, among them the same Marian pilgrimage in Latin and in German.

- [De generatione Christi (1487–1490)](/incunabula/books/de-generatione) — The smallest Ysenhut print; one of its woodcuts returns in the *walfart*.
- [Itinerarius sive peregrinarius (1489)](/incunabula/books/itinerarius-peregrinarius) — Ysenhut's Latin Marian pilgrimage; 220 pages.
- [walfart oder bilgerschafft (1489)](/incunabula/books/walfart) — The German version of the same pilgrimage; shares one woodcut with De generatione and probably another with the Zeitglöcklein (1490).

### Other printers

Four volumes: one each from Bernhard Richel and Jakob Wolff, and two with no named printer – among them the earliest print in the corpus, 1471.

- [Geschichte von der schönen Melusine (1471)](/incunabula/books/melusine) — The earliest print in the corpus; pages numbered consecutively, without signatures, no page descriptions.
- [Itinerarius seu peregrinatio (1475–1499)](/incunabula/books/itinerarius-peregrinatio) — No named printer, no page descriptions; dated only to a quarter-century.
- [Reise ins Heilige Land (1481)](/incunabula/books/reise) — Mandeville's travel account from Richel's office; the same woodcut on leaves 46r and 68r.
- [Die wunderbare Meerfahrt des hl. Brandan (1491)](/incunabula/books/brandan) — Wolff's Brandan legend; two woodcuts appear twice each.
