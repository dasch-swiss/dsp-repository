### Early Basel prints · 1471–1504

# The image sequences of Basel's early prints

### Late-medieval didactics as image-text reading

*Nineteen illustrated early printed books from four Basel printing offices, readable page by page – and the question of how picture and text were read together in the late Middle Ages.*

**I**n the last third of the fifteenth century Basel was the most important centre of early letterpress printing on the territory of today's Switzerland.
What four offices produced here between 1471 and 1504 forms, after the iconoclasm of the Reformation, the most significant surviving Basel image sequence of the late Middle Ages:
nineteen copies carrying over a thousand woodcuts.

The texts are almost all German and Latin compilations of religious and didactic content. Famous books – the *Ship of Fools*, the *Zeitglöcklein* –
sit next to barely known ones such as the pastoral writings of Ludwig Moser and Johannes Meder.

The project's guiding question is the relation of image and text: each print is read as a calculated image-text compilation rather than “a text with illustrations”.
How the woodcuts worked for late-medieval readers and listeners can only be tested on the page itself – which is why every copy here can be opened and turned in full.

## Two ways in {#wege}

- [The Narrenschiff](/incunabula/narrenschiff) — Three editions, one image programme – and the only book with border strips.
- [The re-used woodcut](/incunabula/woodcuts) — One block, printed more than once: what the annotators marked on the scans.

## Timeline {#zeitleiste}

Nineteen volumes, four offices, thirty-three years. Each bar is one book; where only a date range is known, the bar spans every year it could belong to.
