### Three editions · 1495–1497

# The Narrenschiff

*Brant's satire appeared in Basel in 1494. Three Basel editions of 1495 and 1497 from Johann Bergmann de Olpe's office – one German, two Latin – share an image sequence.*

Sebastian Brant's *Narrenschiff* appeared in Basel in 1494; a year later Johann Bergmann de Olpe printed the edition of 3 March 1495.
In 1497 two Latin versions followed in Jacob Locher's translation, in March and in August.

For image research the three volumes are a stroke of luck: where the same block returns, the picture stays while language, typesetting and page order change.
Where a picture stands on another page in the Latin edition, it still stays with its chapter.

The German edition additionally carries a system of narrow border strips framing the text block, absent from the Latin editions.

## Three editions {#editions}

All three were printed by Johann Bergmann de Olpe in Basel: the German on 3 March 1495, the Latin ones on 1 March and 1 August 1497.
The Latin editions open with a letter and a poem from Jacob Locher to Brant, as well as Locher's preface. The German edition is the longest at 328 pages,
the Latin one of March the shortest at 302.

## The border strips {#strips}

The border strips are narrow, reusable ornamental blocks, set to the left and right of the text block and combined in changing pairs.
Thirty-eight types can be told apart: eighteen left, nineteen right and one without a designation. Border strips are attested on 208 pages of the
German edition.

Thirty-one types frame more than eight pages; the most frequent, a right-hand strip, stands on 23.

## The same woodcut in two languages {#reuse}

Seven blocks of the German edition are attested again in the Latin ones, three of them in all three editions, one in four places. Two examples: the
picture for chapter 13, “Von der Buhlerei”, in the German edition and the Latin one of August, and the one for chapter 14, “Von falschen
Vorstellungen über Gott”, in all three.
