### Woodblock and woodcut

# The re-used woodcut

*A woodblock is cut once and printed many times. Where the same block stands in two places, the relation of image and text can be compared directly: the same picture, another place, often another text.*

The same block returns within one volume, travels from the German into the Latin editions of the *Narrenschiff*, and appears in two books from the
same printing office – perhaps even in books from two offices. Each of these repetitions asks the same question: does the picture stay with its text,
or does it stand under a new heading?

The crops are taken from the scans of the copies.

## Between the editions of the Narrenschiff {#editions}

For the Latin version of 1497 the text was typeset anew, and the woodblocks of the German edition of 1495 were used again. Six blocks follow their
chapter from the German into the Latin editions, three of them into all three: the picture for chapter 13, for instance – Venus leads three fools on
ropes, Amor draws his bow blindfolded, Death follows behind Venus – opens the same chapter in the German and in the Latin edition.

In the copy of the German edition, the picture for chapter 7 on b3v carries the handwritten note “peter schellenbeg” and some scribbles.

## Printed four times {#four}

One block, four places – twice in the German edition, twice in the Latin one of August. Here the chapter does change: the picture, a fool with a bird
on his falconer's glove steering a plough that a second fool pulls, stands over chapter 8, “Guten Rat verschmähen”, and
returns over chapter 84, “Vom Festhalten am Guten”. The same picture serves a folly and its opposite; both editions repeat the doubling.

## Inside one book {#within-book}

Within one volume, too, the same block is printed more than once; fourteen such repetitions are attested in seven books. The interval can be a single
leaf or almost the whole book: in Michael Furter's *Postilla* the same picture, Jesus preaching to the disciples, stands on e3r and e4r; in the
German *Narrenschiff* c5v and u1r stand 263 pages apart. There the fools at table stand first over chapter 16, “Von Völlerei und
Prassen”, and then over a new chapter, “Von disches vnzucht”.

The blocks differ greatly in size: the *Postilla* woodcut measures 6.5 × 5.0 cm, that of the fools at table in the *Narrenschiff* 11.7 × 8.4 cm.

## Between books {#between-books}

The blocks travel furthest between books. Whether the *walfart* and Johann Amerbach's *Zeitglöcklein* of 1490 share a block remains open: the
picture in the *walfart*, Judas receiving the thirty pieces of silver from the high priests, is possibly identical with a woodcut in the
*Zeitglöcklein*. There it stands apart from the rest of the series – larger, framed by a single line only, probably made later, perhaps taken over
from another context.

One identity is certain: the Adoration of the Child, 75 × 61 mm, stands in *De generatione Christi* and in the *walfart*, both from Lienhart Ysenhut's
office, coloured in one copy and not in the other. At the end of both Latin editions of the *Narrenschiff*, finally, stands the same printer's device:
a lion as supporter holding the shield of Johann Bergmann de Olpe, above it the inscription “NIHIL .SINE. .CAVSA. 1497.” – the printer's sign, not a
picture to the text.
